# Cardiovascular Chronic Disease Skill: Original and Updated Indicator Comparison

Discussion draft, 2026-09-30. This is a separate updated comparison document. The [original English presentation table](cardiovascular-diseases-table-en.md) remains unchanged. This document corresponds to the [Chinese updated comparison](cardiovascular-diseases-table-updated.md). It compares the 2026-09-23 presentation table and [original full plan](cardiovascular-diseases.md) with the [updated indicator scope](cardiovascular-indicators-updated.md), incorporating the shared [longitudinal management and app integration requirements](longitudinal-app-requirements.md). The full plan, detailed indicator update, and shared requirements linked here are in Chinese.

“Original scope” means content explicitly present in the initial documents. “Additions or refinements” expands that scope; existing fields remain included. Conditions previously grouped under specialist reports now have explicit fields, but are not newly added diseases. These are records the app should support, not a universal testing panel or a statement that the features or clinical decision rules have been implemented.

## Purpose and Population

| Question | Current plan |
| --- | --- |
| Who is it for? | An AD or other existing diagnosis is not required. Adult patients may also have PD, cancer, metabolic disease, or no established chronic disease diagnosis. Pediatric and pregnancy applicability requires separate review. |
| What does it do? | Assess cardiovascular risks and follow-up gaps when relevant information is available; support condition-specific long-term management after clinical confirmation. |
| How are states separated? | Record risk factors, abnormal findings awaiting confirmation, confirmed disease, and prior acute events separately. |
| Proposed full first-version support | Adult hypertension, chronic coronary disease, heart failure, and atrial fibrillation. Expanding the field catalog below does not automatically expand implementation commitments. |
| Expansion catalog | Peripheral artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, valvular disease, other arrhythmias, aortic aneurysm, adult congenital heart disease, long-term care after venous thromboembolism, and rheumatic heart disease. Initially retain confirmed diagnoses, reports, and established plans; implement rules individually. |
| Less common or rare-disease catalog | Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, pulmonary arterial hypertension, inherited long QT syndrome, Brugada syndrome, and familial thoracic aortic aneurysm. Initially retain and display specialist records without applying generic hypertension or heart-failure rules. These conditions are not all extremely rare. |
| Cross-condition boundaries | Hypertension is both a managed condition and a risk factor for other cardiovascular diseases. Long-term prevention after myocardial infarction or stroke is included; post-stroke neurological management requires coordination with the neurology lead. |

## Indicator Comparison Across 18 Conditions

This table summarizes field changes. See the [detailed indicator update](cardiovascular-indicators-updated.md) for applicability, clinical sources, and disease-specific evidence gaps. Original fields remain included even when not repeated in the additions column.

| Condition | Original scope | Additions or refinements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypertension | Paired blood pressure readings, time, home or clinic source, device, measurement context, and personal target; repeated measurements and long-term trends. | Specify posture, measurement side, cuff, and rest conditions; retain available daytime, nighttime, and 24-hour ambulatory results. Add initial-assessment records for blood count, electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, lipids, glucose or HbA1c, TSH, urinalysis, UACR, and ECG. A single home reading still does not establish a diagnosis or justify a treatment change. |
| Chronic coronary disease | Diagnostic evidence; chest discomfort and changes; myocardial infarction, stent, or bypass history; exercise tolerance, ECG, functional testing, and coronary imaging. | Specify symptom timing, duration, activity or rest context, frequency, relief, and functional impact. Specify the vessel, location, stenosis severity, imaging method, and date. Retain troponin for relevant acute assessments rather than routine trend monitoring in stable disease. |
| Heart failure | Confirmed type, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), serial weight, breathlessness, edema, fatigue, and functional changes; available BNP/NT-proBNP, kidney function, and electrolytes. | Add historical echocardiography dates and methods, dated NYHA class, heart-failure admissions, and available six-minute walk results. Expand initial-assessment records, explicitly including ferritin and transferrin saturation (TSAT). Link medication changes to follow-up measurements; weight change alone does not establish fluid retention or justify changing treatment. |
| Atrial fibrillation | ECG or medical-record confirmation, episode pattern, heart rate, symptoms, stroke/TIA history, clinician-assessed risks, and anticoagulant prescription; missed doses, bleeding, and kidney function. | Add confirmation source, monitoring start/end, episode duration, and AF burden with its denominator. Retain risk-tool version, inputs, and assessment date. Add blood count, metabolic panel, thyroid function, and transthoracic echocardiography records at initial diagnosis. Use INR according to actual medication; wearable alerts do not automatically become confirmed diagnoses. |
| Peripheral artery disease | Walking-induced leg symptoms, foot wounds, ankle-brachial index (ABI), and established plans. | Distinguish left/right and resting/post-exercise ABI, retaining ankle and arm pressures. Add toe-brachial index (TBI), toe pressure, and perfusion data when indicated. Add walking distance, rest pain, wound healing, vascular imaging, and revascularization records. |
| Cerebrovascular disease and long-term post-stroke care | Confirmed event type, date, and post-discharge prevention plan; coordination with neurology. | Specify cause and diagnostic certainty, brain and head/neck vascular imaging, AF screening, and antithrombotic indication. Retain dates and assessors for existing NIHSS and mRS scores, plus swallowing, mobility, and daily functioning. Handle ischemic and hemorrhagic events separately. |
| Valvular heart disease | Specific valve, lesion type, severity, and original echocardiography report. | Specify valve area, velocity, pressure gradient, or regurgitation measurements according to the valve and lesion. Add native/prosthetic status, chamber and cardiac function, procedure date, and valve type. Select parameters by lesion; do not automatically grade severity from a single gradient. |
| Other arrhythmias and conduction disorders | Rhythm records, key reports, and follow-up plans within the expansion catalog. | Specify the clinician-confirmed subtype, PR/QRS/QT/QTc, monitoring duration, episode count and duration, premature ventricular contraction burden, pauses, or conduction block. Retain symptom timing and pacemaker/ICD events. Each subtype still requires separate rule review. |
| Aortic aneurysm | Imaging location, diameter, and follow-up interval. | Add anatomical segment, maximum diameter, measurement plane, method, and date. Calculate growth only from comparable measurements and display the inputs. Add repair history, residual disease, and post-endograft reports; separate device dimensions from aneurysm diameter. |
| Adult congenital heart disease | Confirmed diagnosis, key reports, and established follow-up plans without fields for individual defects. | Add the specific defect, original anatomy, repair history, residual lesions, and specialist team. Retain oxygen saturation, ventricular and valve function, shunt direction, Qp/Qs, and available catheterization data according to the defect. Distinguish before/after repair and different measurement methods. |
| Long-term care after venous thromboembolism | Confirmed diagnosis, key reports, and established follow-up plans. | Specify DVT/PE type, location, date, first or recurrent event, confirming imaging, and provoking factors. Add anticoagulant start/interruption, treatment-duration review, missed doses, bleeding, and persistent symptoms. D-dimer and residual-thrombus ultrasound are not mandatory stopping-treatment tests for every patient. |
| Rheumatic heart disease | Diagnosis, reports, and plans within the expansion catalog. | Add rheumatic fever and recurrence history, valve involvement, and related events. Retain prescribed secondary prophylaxis, actual administration, missed doses, allergy, and adverse effects. Share relevant examination records with the valvular module. |
| Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy | Confirmed name, specialist imaging, rhythm or genetic records, care team, and plan. | Specify maximum wall thickness and segment, LVEF, left ventricular outflow tract gradients under different conditions, late gadolinium enhancement on CMR, and apical aneurysm. Add syncope, arrhythmias, family history, genetic classification, and ICD events. Store resting and provoked gradients separately. |
| Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy | Confirmed diagnosis and specialist imaging, rhythm, and genetic records without explicit fields. | Add right/left ventricular phenotype, ejection fractions, volumes and body-size indexing, regional motion, and fibrosis. Retain premature ventricular contraction burden, ventricular tachycardia, exercise exposure, genetic records, and ICD events. Preserve distinct phenotypes. |
| Pulmonary arterial hypertension | Confirmed diagnosis, specialist reports, care team, and follow-up plan. | Specify right-heart catheterization mean pulmonary artery pressure, wedge pressure, pulmonary vascular resistance, cardiac output, cardiac index, and right atrial pressure, with methods. Add WHO functional class, walk distance, BNP/NT-proBNP, and right-heart records. Separate echocardiographic estimates from catheter measurements. |
| Inherited long QT syndrome | Confirmed diagnosis, relevant rhythm or genetic records, and follow-up plan. | Retain QT, QTc, correction formula, concurrent heart rate or RR interval, and date separately. Add exercise/recovery findings, event triggers, family history, relevant medications, and electrolyte context. Separate inherited and acquired states. |
| Brugada syndrome | Confirmed diagnosis, specialist ECG or genetic reports, and plan. | Specify ECG pattern, lead position, fever status, and spontaneous/provoked context. Add symptoms, family history, clinical interpretation of provocation testing, and confirmed ICD events. Keep recorded patterns distinct from confirmed syndrome status. |
| Familial thoracic aortic aneurysm | Confirmed diagnosis, imaging, genetic records, care team, and follow-up plan. | Add affected relatives' relationships, disease locations, ages at onset, and family imaging plans. Specify gene, variant, transcript, zygosity, pathogenicity classification, and report date. A variant of uncertain significance does not automatically establish the cause. |

## Shared Data and Rule Comparison

| Topic | Original scope | Additions or refinements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared indicators | Blood pressure, heart rate, weight, waist, glucose, HbA1c, lipids, kidney function, lifestyle, and a unified medication list. | Add height, traceable BMI calculation, blood count, electrolytes, urinalysis/UACR, liver tests, TSH, and iron studies. Read them according to disease and treatment needs; do not require all fields for every patient. |
| Analytes and units | Record fields, units, and sources. | Separate creatinine, eGFR, and creatinine clearance; UACR, urine protein/creatinine ratio, and urinalysis protein; ferritin, serum iron, and TSAT; and BNP from NT-proBNP. Preserve original values, units, and conversion basis. |
| Examination purpose and status | Distinguish risk, abnormal findings awaiting confirmation, confirmed disease, and events; identify missing data. | Specify initial assessment, stable follow-up, assessment of symptom changes, medication monitoring, or postoperative follow-up. Distinguish applicability, ordering, completion, pending results, negative findings, unavailable results, and conflicting source text. Missing information does not automatically mean a normal result or an omitted examination. |
| Evidence and provenance | Clinical-source links and traceable outputs. | Distinguish guideline support, patient-record support, case-report support, and evidence gaps. These are not evidence grades and do not establish universal applicability. A procedure record cannot substitute for its actual result. |
| Data types and history | Serial measurements, symptoms, goals, and treatment plans support long-term management; the app stores the records. | Distinguish diagnostic records, repeated observations, and clinical/treatment events. Append new observations and retain history; preserve correction relationships and exclude invalidated records from current analysis. |
| Time information | Record dates and measurement context. | Separate measurement, note, entry, and event times, preserving their actual precision. Historical assessments must not use information that was unavailable at the assessment time. |
| Trend interpretation | Summarize long-term trends in disease context. | Check analyte, specimen, units, method, and context before comparing. Report the observation window, valid record count, and time span. Two measurements can show a difference; claims such as a persistent rise require disease-specific rules. |
| Goals and treatment changes | Retain personal goals, prescriptions, actual use, and symptom/treatment relationships. | Preserve the source, effective time, and history of goals and treatment plans. Associate results with treatment changes and admissions. Temporal association does not automatically establish a medication effect. |
| Missing and outdated information | Report insufficient information; a missing test is not a normal test. | Show the last result date, available history, and gaps. No new records must not be interpreted as stability. Determine follow-up timing from clinician plans or reviewed rules. |
| Multimorbidity and reminders | Share observations, merge duplicate reminders, and surface potential conflicts. | Update reminders when results arrive, plans change, or issues are resolved. Do not count the same observation twice in a trend. Retain each Skill's reasoning and route conflicts for review. |
| App inputs and outputs | The app stores patient records; Skills store reusable knowledge. Outputs carry evidence, dates, and versions. | Supply the assessment time, new or corrected observations, relevant history, treatment events, and effective goals. Return comparable trends, current state, gaps, and next-step prompts. Concrete interfaces remain to be implemented. |
| Testing | Synthetic cases cover risk states, confirmed disease, and multimorbidity. | Add longitudinal sequences covering duplicates, out-of-order entry, missing dates, unit/device changes, prolonged gaps, goal changes, treatment adjustments, corrections, and reminder updates. |

The existing medication-knowledge scope remains: classes and purposes of antihypertensives, lipid-lowering agents, antiplatelets, anticoagulants, and heart-failure therapies. The app maintains actual prescriptions and use in a shared record. Complex interactions, anticoagulant selection, and dose changes require the clinical team or validated resources. Acute findings still enter the shared safety process before routine trend analysis; sufficient longitudinal data are not a prerequisite.

The formal Skill package still needs disease names and synonyms, field and unit dictionaries, medication knowledge, approved educational materials, clinical sources and versions, applicability criteria, and synthetic test cases. Actual patient records remain in patient storage rather than reusable Skill instructions.

## Evidence and Implementation Status

| Item | Current status |
| --- | --- |
| Field planning | The 18 conditions now have individual field descriptions, supplemented by shared fields, examination context, provenance, and quality information. |
| Patient-data support | The original 50-patient field audit and [expanded evidence review](../cardiovascular-evidence-review/README.md) describe support and gaps by condition. This does not mean there are 50 patients per condition or that all added fields have been verified in patient data. |
| Longitudinal validation | Field audits, additional datasets, and cases do not establish effective longitudinal management for each condition. Cross-visit linkage, valid time points, follow-up duration, and missingness require separate assessment. |
| App implementation | This table and the shared longitudinal requirements are planning documents. They do not establish that storage, invocation, trend analysis, or reminders have been implemented and validated. |
| Clinical rules | Diagnostic thresholds, rules for setting individual targets, follow-up intervals, medication doses, automatic risk scores, and urgent-alert criteria are not yet executable rules. Each requires applicability, exclusions, source versions, and clinical review before activation. |
| Cross-team ownership | Shared data and reminder merging are planned. Maintenance responsibilities across disease categories still require team agreement; suggestions made in discussion are not approved ownership rules. |

Disease-specific clinical references and detailed limitations are in the [indicator update](cardiovascular-indicators-updated.md). Shared timeline, input/output, and acceptance requirements are in the [longitudinal app requirements](longitudinal-app-requirements.md).
